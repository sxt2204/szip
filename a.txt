SECTION 1: THE ESSENCE OF DATA COMPRESSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Data compression is the art and science of representing information in a more compact form. At its core, data compression exploits redundancy in the underlying source material. Redundancy can manifest in various ways, such as spatial redundancy in images, temporal redundancy in video streams, or statistical redundancy in text files.

There are two primary categories of data compression: lossy and lossless.
Lossless compression guarantees that the original data can be reconstructed perfectly from the compressed representation. This is crucial for executable files, source code, medical imagery, and textual databases where a single altered bit can invalidate the whole dataset. Lossy compression, on the other hand, discards perceptually irrelevant or less important information to achieve dramatically higher compression ratios, making it ideal for streaming audio, photos, and consumer video.

In modern computer science, algorithms like LZ77, LZ78, LZW, Huffman Coding, Arithmetic Coding, and Asymmetric Numeral Systems (ANS) form the backbone of state-of-the-art compression tools. LZ77 works by substituting repeated occurrences of data with references to a single copy existing earlier in the uncompressed data stream (often referred to as a sliding window). When combined with entropy coders like Huffman trees or Range Coders, LZ77 yields incredible efficiency, as seen in DEFLATE (used in ZIP and gzip), Brotli, and Zstandard.
